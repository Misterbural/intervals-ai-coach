# Science Watch: Current Evidence Benchmarks

Baseline version (v0). Verify, expand, and update during your first full review (`coach-block-brief`), then refresh every 3–6 months or whenever switching to a new sport or major event format. These benchmarks supersede `endurance-methodology.md` when updated.

## Entry Format
- **Benchmark**: rule currently applied
- **Evidence level**: strong (meta-analyses, consensus) / moderate (multiple consistent studies) / low (isolated studies, field heuristics)
- **Sources**
- **Last verified**: date
- **What would change it**

---

## Carbohydrate Intake During Exercise
- **Benchmark**: < 1 h easy/moderate: water only; ~1 h intense: 0–30 g/h (carb mouth rinse effective); 1–2.5 h: 30–60 g/h; > 2.5 h: up to 90 g/h glucose-fructose blend; progressive gut training for long events (90–120 g/h only if well tolerated).
- **Evidence level**: strong up to 90 g/h; > 90 g/h debated and highly individual.
- **Sources**: ACSM/AND/DC Nutrition Consensus 2016; Jeukendrup 2014; GSSI Sports Science Exchange; Morton et al., *J Nutr* 2026.
- **Last verified**: baseline.
- **What would change it**: stronger randomized data on exogenous oxidation > 90 g/h and GI tolerance in running/trail.

## Pre-Session Fueling
- **Benchmark**: carbohydrate-rich meal 1–4 h before any key session lasting > 1 h.
- **Evidence level**: strong (sports nutrition consensus).
- **Sources**: ACSM Consensus 2016; Jeukendrup 2014.
- **Last verified**: baseline.

## Energy Availability (REDs)
- **Benchmark**: no deliberate energy restriction during injury return or heavy training blocks; watch for low energy availability signs (persistent fatigue, declining performance, bone/tendon stress, poor sleep); refer weight-loss goals to a sports dietitian.
- **Evidence level**: strong (IOC consensus).
- **Sources**: Mountjoy et al., IOC Consensus Statement on REDs, *Br J Sports Med* 2023.
- **Last verified**: baseline.

## Strength Training & Plyometrics for Endurance
- **Benchmark**: 2 sessions/week during base, 1 near A-races; when paired with endurance on the same day, aim for ≥ 3 h separation and avoid within 2 days before a VO2max session.
- **Evidence level**: moderate for economy/efficiency gains via **heavy resistance training (≥ 80% 1RM)** and/or **plyometrics** (running); low-to-moderate for minimal-equipment home strength on direct race performance (still valuable for durability, force, and injury prevention).
- **Sources**: Llanos-Lagos et al., *Eur J Appl Physiol* 2025 (cycling); Llanos-Lagos et al., *Sports Med* 2024 (running economy); Schumann et al., *Sports Med* 2022 (concurrent training).
- **Last verified**: baseline.

## Intensity Distribution
- **Benchmark**: pyramidal during base, polarized optional during build; avoid dogma—total volume, consistency, and avoiding unintentional "gray zone" fatigue matter more.
- **Evidence level**: moderate.
- **Sources**: Oliveira, Boppre & Fonseca, *Sports Med* 2024; Rosenblat et al., *Sports Med* 2025 (individual-participant network meta-analysis).
- **Last verified**: baseline.

## Tapering
- **Benchmark**: full taper reserved for A-races; 10–14 days (pre-taper from D-21); progressive 40–60% volume reduction while preserving intensity and session frequency.
- **Evidence level**: strong (meta-analysis) for volume reduction; low for exact race-day TSB target windows (field conventions).
- **Sources**: Bosquet et al., *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2007; Mujika & Padilla 2003.
- **Last verified**: baseline.

## Durability (Fatigue Resistance)
- **Benchmark**: monitor the ability to sustain power or pace under accumulated work (Pa:Hr decoupling, fatigued power curves in kJ for cycling, late-long-run fade in running/trail); prioritize durability when repeatedly degraded.
- **Evidence level**: moderate (established determinant of long-endurance performance distinct from fresh VO2max/threshold; optimal training protocols remain under investigation).
- **Sources**: Maunder et al., *Sports Med* 2021; Spragg, Leo & Swart, *Eur J Sport Sci* 2023; Valenzuela et al., *Int J Sports Physiol Perform* 2023; Hamilton et al. 2024; Clark et al. 2018.
- **Last verified**: baseline.

## HRV & Resting HR Daily Readiness
- **Benchmark**: 7-day rolling mean of ln(rMSSD) compared to mean ± 0.5 SD of the preceding 28 nights (excluding last 7 days); RHR 7-day mean and last night compared to the same non-overlapping baseline (> +3 bpm 7d trend or ≥ +5 bpm single-night spike = flag).
- **Evidence level**: moderate for HRV-guided endurance training; low for fixed RHR cutoffs (heuristics to recalibrate individually).
- **Sources**: Plews et al.; Vesterinen et al., *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2016.
- **Last verified**: baseline.

## Monotony, Strain & Exclusion of ACWR
- **Benchmark**: use Foster monotony and strain as complementary weekly indicators (monotony > 2 flags overly uniform weeks); intentionally exclude ACWR from decision-making.
- **Evidence level**: low-to-moderate for Foster heuristics; moderate for methodological critique of ACWR.
- **Sources**: Foster, *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 1998; Impellizzeri et al., *Int J Sports Physiol Perform* 2020; Impellizzeri et al., *Sports Med* 2021.
- **Last verified**: baseline.

## Heat & Acclimation
- **Benchmark**: above ~23 °C, HR drifts higher at equal output; 1–2 weeks of heat acclimation before hot races; sodium-rich hydration and strong airflow indoors.
- **Evidence level**: strong (consensus).
- **Sources**: Racinais et al., Consensus Recommendations on Training and Competing in the Heat, *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 2015.
- **Last verified**: baseline.
